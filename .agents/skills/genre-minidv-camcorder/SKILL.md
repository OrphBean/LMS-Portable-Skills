# genre-minidv-camcorder

Use when rendering early-2000s consumer MiniDV camcorder footage: small-sensor digital video, body-driven handheld motion with inertia, imperfect reactive framing, occasional optical rocker zoom, built-in mic. This is the default camera behaviour unless the user's scene facts require otherwise. It reads as genuine consumer digital video, not film, not VHS.

---

## Style reference block

Early-2000s consumer MiniDV camcorder footage. Small-sensor digital-video appearance with slightly washed-out colour, restrained saturation, pale highlights, modest dynamic range, mild electronic edge sharpening, fine video noise, occasional clipped bright areas, and broad depth of field - like genuine consumer digital video rather than modern cinema, film emulation, or VHS. The camera behaves like a compact handheld MiniDV camcorder operated by a person moving through the scene. The built-in lens stays moderately wide (roughly 35-45mm full-frame equivalent). The operator stays physically close to the subject, so camera position, perspective and background parallax change visibly as both move. Handheld motion follows human body mechanics: walking gives gentle vertical rise and fall, small lateral shoulder sway, slight rotational roll, irregular impact from footsteps; faster movement gives larger but coherent bumps. The camera has inertia - it accelerates, overshoots slightly, settles rather than changing direction instantly. Framing is reactive and imperfect: the subject drifts toward the frame edge, loses ideal headroom, or moves briefly off-centre before the operator corrects. When the subject changes direction the camera reacts slightly late, the operator makes a quick physical pan, briefly overshoots, then corrects back. The built-in optical zoom is used occasionally and deliberately, as a rocker zoom: the field of view narrows or widens from the fixed camera position (perspective stays constant - never a dolly/push-in), over a visible duration with mechanical continuity, and only when the operator plausibly inspects, isolates, or reacquires something. Preserve the spontaneous observational feel: reactive decisions, imperfect composition, late reframing, visible body-driven movement, short rocker zooms, close physical proximity.

## Trait schema

| Attribute | This genre |
| --- | --- |
| Capture | Early-2000s small-sensor digital video; washed color, pale highlights, mild edge sharpening |
| Camera | Compact handheld camcorder; moderately wide lens (35-45mm FFE); operator nearby |
| Motion | Body-driven inertia; rise/fall, sway, roll, footstep impact; overshoot then settle |
| Framing | Reactive and imperfect; drifting headroom, off-centre, late corrections |
| Zoom | Optical rocker zoom; visible duration, fixed perspective; only to inspect/isolate/reacquire |
| Optics | Broad depth of field; telephoto magnifies handheld shake |
| Audio | Small consumer camcorder mic; close footsteps, clothing, local voices, handling noise |
| Mood | Spontaneous, observational, candid; invented flaws are the style, not bugs |

## Exclusions

- No modern cinema, no film stock, no VHS tracking/static.
- No constant uniform shake or floating gimbal motion (body-driven inertia with late, imperfect corrections instead).
- No zoom read as a dolly or push-in - perspective must stay fixed; only the field of view changes.
- No zoom without an in-scene reason (inspect, isolate, reacquire).
- No "perfect" or "stable" description anywhere.
- Never override an explicit user camera request (e.g. a named slow dolly) - keep the MiniDV look and audio but preserve the user's camera work.

## Example

Early-2000s consumer MiniDV camcorder footage, small-sensor digital-video appearance with restrained saturation and fine video noise, the camera held by an operator walking close alongside a woman crossing a quiet street, moderate wide field of view, gentle vertical rise and fall with small shoulder sway, the woman drifts briefly toward the frame edge before a short late pan corrects, a short optical rocker zoom-in isolates her face as she stops, perspective stays fixed while the field narrows and handheld shake grows slightly, then the operator zooms back out as she moves on, natural location sound through a small consumer microphone with close footsteps and a faint handling bump.
