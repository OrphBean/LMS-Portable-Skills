# genre-vhs-domestic

Use when rendering an ordinary 1990s family VHS recording: consumer camcorder footage, 4:3 interlaced analog video, household tungsten light, autoexposure/autofocus errors, in-camera hard cuts, built-in microphone. Choose exactly one domestic event type (Trait schema) and write the social situation before the camera behavior.

---

## Style reference block

1990s consumer VHS home video, 4:3 interlaced analog video recorded on a consumer camcorder by a family member, first-generation tape. The image is soft and electronically processed rather than filmic: low-resolution luminance, noticeably poorer chroma, reds and saturated blues that bleed past object edges, white highlights that bloom and smear, and shadow-region video noise. Warm tungsten household light gives skin a peach or slightly yellow cast, with blown windows in the background and autoexposure that lags and pulses. Handheld motion is irregular and human, not gimbal-smooth: slow reactive pans, late reframing, occasional overshoot, and zooming instead of walking closer. The operator stays at adult standing eye height, composing loosely and losing ideal headroom; subjects drift toward the frame edge before a correction. People acknowledge the camera: waving, posing, addressing the operator, or asking "is that thing on?" Focus briefly hunts to the background or to bright ornaments, then recovers. Cut with hard in-camera edits and temporal jumps, no coverage continuity, a built-in camcorder microphone capturing room echo, nearby voices, and the operator's own laugh over the subject. Keep it clean and watchable - not broken. No cinematic lighting, no shallow depth of field, no gimbal, no post-produced montage.

## Trait schema

| Camera | Handheld from adult standing eye height; family member operates |

| Framing | Loose medium/wide groups, cramped rooms, household clutter in frame |
| Color | Warm tungsten; peach skin; chroma bleed in reds/saturated colors |
| Editing | In-camera hard cuts; long fragments; no coverage continuity |

Event type (pick exactly one, the dominant; others are secondary at most): everyday-family, birthday-party, christmas-morning, family-dinner, vacation, backyard-summer, school-event, baby-toddler, teen-bedroom, pet, home-tour, family-reunion. Each has its own movement/framing/palette/audio nuance; the matrix and per-type tags live in `references/<slug>-source.md`. Write "recorded by <who> from <where>" to anchor the social behaviour.

## Exclusions

- No 35mm film, no cinematic 24fps, no film grain, no shallow depth of field.
- No gimbal/drone, no smooth digital push-in, no DSLR bokeh.
- No stylised "destroyed VHS": constant tracking errors, giant static bands, heavy glitching are NOT the norm.
- No post-production shot/reverse-shot montage; no music-video cutting.
- No modern smartphone autofocus, no perfect neutral white balance, no teal-orange grade, no HDR.
- No curated retro museum set - household clutter should be ordinary and only incidentally period-specific.
- No fake universal date overlay on every shot.

## Example

1994 New Zealand suburban family home video, recorded by a family member on a PAL consumer VHS camcorder, 4:3 interlaced analog video, an ordinary living room after dinner, loose handheld medium-wide framing from standing adult eye height, two relatives talking on a couch while a child moves in and out of the foreground, the camera reacts slightly late and pans after the child has already crossed frame, brief motorized zoom into one speaker then an unnecessary zoom back out, autofocus momentarily catches the curtain behind them, warm tungsten light giving skin a peach-yellow cast, a blown white window in the background, beige carpet, dark timber cabinet, dusty blue upholstery and ordinary clutter, family members occasionally look toward the camera and address the person filming, built-in microphone captures room echo, the television and the operator laughing, soft VHS chroma, mild red bleed, noisy shadow detail, stable first-generation tape.
