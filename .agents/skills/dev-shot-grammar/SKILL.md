# dev-shot-grammar

Use when choosing how to frame each beat of a sequence, or when checking continuity across cuts. Supply the grammar, not the creative content; pick the framing that carries the beat's job. Do NOT use for environment/lighting (dev-mise-en-scene), genre (genre-orchestrator), or producing H3 syntax.

---

## Reasoning mode

`instruct` — the mappings are deterministic decision tables. No reasoning trace; do not over-think a framing choice.

## Shot size — map to what it must achieve

| Size | Narrative job | Frame reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extreme long | establish scale / majesty / location geography | tiny subjects, vast area |
| Long | establish the action area and actor positions | full scene, actors small but readable |
| Medium | the body of the scene; reader/viewer at middle distance | actor knees-up / waist-up |
| Medium close-up | social and gesture detail | waist to above head |
| Close-up | emotional emphasis / a meaning-bearing detail | head-and-shoulders or head |
| Extreme close-up | isolate a detail (eyes, hands, object) | a single feature |
| Insert | full-screen of a written/printed/precise detail | letter, sign, hand |

- Frame decision is relative to **the subject's own image size**, not camera distance or lens.
- Match height: a close-up of a seated subject must be at that subject's eye level.
- Establish with wide, then move closer; return to a long shot when actor positions become narratively meaningful.

## Camera angle — map to power/relationship

| Angle | Psychological effect |
| --- | --- |
| Eye-level (level) | neutral, objective, natural |
| High angle | makes the subject small / vulnerable / dominated |
| Low angle | confers power, authority, or supernatural dominance |
| Dutch / canted | disorientation, intoxication, unstable subjectivity |
| Over-the-shoulder | establishes two-party relation; presence of both |
| Point-of-view | viewer stands beside the subject (objective) or in their eyes (subjective) |

- Subject angle at a ¾ (about 45°) view reads most three-dimensional and flattering; avoid dead-on flat frontal or pure profile unless a reason.
- For dialogue, seat the two at eye level (adjust for height differences; drop to the seated/child eye level, not the standing operator's).

## Camera movement — map to motivation

| Move | Motivation |
| --- | --- |
| Dolly / push-in | intensify attention, recognition, control taking hold |
| Dolly out | reveal, detach, scale, isolate the subject in context |
| Pan | follow a moving subject, or scan space |
| Truck / lateral track | accompany a subject, keep profile |
| Pedestal / crane | rise/fall, reveal vertical scale, change emphasis |
| Zoom | change field of view from a fixed position (never a substitute for physical dolly) |
| Handheld | immediacy, instability, documentary rawness |
| Shake | distress, violence, impact |

One principal camera move per shot; give it direction, amplitude, speed, and what framing it arrives at. A move must be explainable from the action (a push-in follows a glance).

## Composition elements (guide the eye)

- Framing conventions that pull attention: rule of thirds, leading lines, frame-within-a-frame (doorway/window/arch), foreground layers for depth, balance (symmetry or intentional asymmetry), negative space for isolation, and colour/tone as accent.
- Use depth via foreground/middle/background layering and overlapping subjects; angle the camera to get two surfaces of the subject.
- Headroom and look-space matter; keep the subject's eyeline side consistent.

## Continuity and cutting

- Preserve the **axis of action** and **screen direction** across cuts; a character exiting left should enter on the same side next.
- Match on action: a cut during movement must resume at the same physical phase, not restart.
- Keep the **180° rule** for two characters; a reversal crosses the axis only when intended.
- Coverage order: establishing → medium blocking → shot/reverse-shot → reaction close-up → insert or key detail.
- Cut to introduce new information (subject, space, state, viewpoint, time); otherwise prefer camera motion over a cut.
- Use cut-in (into a part of the same space), cut-away (a related event elsewhere), reaction shot (silent response).

## Transitions

Hard cut (default), dissolve (time/dream passage), fade (ending/beginnings), wipe (stylized), match cut (shape/action association). Use a transition only to signal a temporal or conceptual change, not decoration.

## Decision rules

- Prefer a continuous causal action chain over a list of independent actions for a short clip.
- Once a direction is established, maintain it unless the subject visibly turns around.
- Never specify a framing that contradicts the beat's narrative job.

## Failure modes

- Choosing a shot size by distance/lens instead of subject image size.
- Shooting a seated subject from standing eye level (produces a bad high-angle close-up).
- A reverse shot that breaks the axis of action unannounced.
- A zoom described as if it were a dolly (perspective must stay fixed).
- An unmotivated camera move, or two competing moves in one shot.
- Blocking that reads as a mood instead of concrete subjects behaving causally.
